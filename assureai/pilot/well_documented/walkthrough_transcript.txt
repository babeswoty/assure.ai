Control: PILOT-A-UAM-001 - New User Access Provisioning Approval
Interviewee: T. Nkosi, IT Security Analyst (fictitious person)

[L01] Interviewer: Can you walk me through how a new employee gets system access?
[L02] Interviewee: Sure. When someone joins, their manager submits an access request form through the ServiceNow portal.
[L03] Interviewee: The form lists the specific systems and access level needed based on the new hire's job role.
[L04] Interviewer: Who reviews that request?
[L05] Interviewee: I do, along with one of my colleagues in IT Security. We check the requested access against the standard access profile for that role before provisioning anything.
[L06] Interviewer: How often does this happen, or is it triggered by something specific?
[L07] Interviewee: It's triggered every time HR notifies us of a new starter, so it happens continuously, not on a schedule. We aim to provision within 2 business days of the start date.
[L08] Interviewer: Why do you check against a standard access profile rather than just granting what the manager asks for?
[L09] Interviewee: Because managers don't always know the technical access needed, and this is our way of preventing over-provisioning or granting access outside someone's role. It's a control against a manager just rubber-stamping whatever the new employee's predecessor had.
[L10] Interviewer: Where is all this recorded?
[L11] Interviewee: Everything lives in ServiceNow - the request form, our approval, and the provisioning ticket are all in the same record, timestamped.
[L12] Interviewer: And how exactly do you provision the access once it's approved?
[L13] Interviewee: I create the account in Active Directory using our standard role-based templates, then assign the specific application groups listed on the approved request. I take a screenshot of the final group membership and attach it to the ServiceNow ticket.
