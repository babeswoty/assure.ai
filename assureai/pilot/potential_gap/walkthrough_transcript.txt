Control: PILOT-D-CM-004 - Emergency Change Post-Implementation Approval
Interviewee: P. Naidoo, Change Manager (fictitious person)

[L01] Interviewer: Walk me through what happens when an emergency change is needed.
[L02] Interviewee: If something's broken in production and it can't wait for the normal change window, the engineer deploys the fix immediately and logs it as an emergency change in our change management tool.
[L03] Interviewer: Who is involved, and when does approval happen relative to the deployment?
[L04] Interviewee: The engineer deploys first, since it's an emergency, and then the change is supposed to go to the CAB - that's myself and two other senior engineers - for approval within 2 business days after the fact.
[L05] Interviewer: Why is approval after the fact rather than before, for these specific changes?
[L06] Interviewee: Because waiting for a CAB meeting before fixing a production outage would make the outage worse. The point is to allow the fix to happen fast, then have independent eyes confirm it was appropriate shortly afterward.
[L07] Interviewer: Where is this recorded?
[L08] Interviewee: In the change management tool - there's a ticket for the emergency change itself, and a separate approval record once CAB signs off.
[L09] Interviewer: How does the CAB actually perform that post-implementation review?
[L10] Interviewee: We look at what was changed, why, whether it worked, and whether it should also go through a proper change request afterward for the permanent fix.
[L11] Interviewer: How reliably does the 2-day approval SLA actually get met in practice?
[L12] Interviewee: Honestly, not that reliably. I pulled the log before this call out of curiosity - of the last 6 emergency changes this quarter, only 2 have a completed CAB approval record. The other 4 are still sitting open, some of them over three weeks old.
[L13] Interviewer: Is there any escalation or tracking for those open ones?
[L14] Interviewee: Not really - there's no automatic reminder or escalation, it relies on one of us remembering to follow up.
